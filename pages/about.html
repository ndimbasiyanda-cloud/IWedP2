HISTORY OF THE CHURCH

The Reformed Church in South Africa has a rich history that reflects the diverse influences and movements within the Christian community. The church was formed in response to doctrinal and liturgical domination within the Reformed Sister churches of South Africa, particularly the Nederduitse Hervormde Kerk (NHK). The Reformed Church in South Africa, also known as the Reformed Churches in South Africa (RCSA), was founded in 1859 and is known for its conservative stance on certain hymns and doctrines. The church has undergone significant changes over the years, including the formation of the Uniting Reformed Church in Southern Africa (URCSA) and the Free Reformed Churches in South Africa (FRCSA). These changes have been driven by the need for unity, reconciliation, and justice within the church community. The Reformed Church in South Africa continues to play a vital role in the religious landscape of the country, with its members often referred to as Doppers.
